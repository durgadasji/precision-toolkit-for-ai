# Frame Language Decomposition: Implementation Guide

## What this guide is, and what it is not

This is an implementation guide. It is how to decompose a domain that has no agreed structure into the structure underneath it, written for someone who will use the method rather than refine it.

Decomposition is the companion to the vocabulary check. The vocabulary check takes an existing account and holds its terms to their meaning. Decomposition comes earlier: you have a domain, a practice, a tangle of phenomena with no settled vocabulary at all, and you need to surface what is actually structuring it before you can name anything. The method takes the visible surface of the domain and works downward to the conditions that generate it, until you have a minimal basis: a small set of structural conditions from which the whole surface can be rebuilt.

Two things have to be held from the start. First, the method reaches optimal Frame 2 and stops there. It surfaces the structural conditions, the buildable basis, and it does not reach the source any more than the other tools do; you cannot compose or decompose your way to Frame 3. Second, and specific to this tool, the check that the structure you surfaced is actually in the domain rather than projected onto it is a human judgment, not a mechanical step. The decomposition to best Frame 2 can be run by a machine; the orientation check cannot.

## When to reach for it

Reach for decomposition when you face a domain or construct with no agreed structure and you need one: a new kind of program with no vocabulary, a practice that works but has never been specified, a field where every account is somebody's private model. In the toolkit it is the entry for naming or decomposing a domain that has no agreed vocabulary, and it is the step that produces the structure the later tools then grind, cut, and name.

It does not fit when the domain already has a sound structure and you only need to check its words; that is the vocabulary check. And it does not fit when what you actually want is a verdict on whether the domain is good or right; decomposition surfaces what is there, not what it is worth.

## How to run it

1. Surface pass, Frame 1. Collect the visible expressions of the domain without theorizing them yet: the actual behaviors, artifacts, roles, events, records. This is the surface, and you want it as fully and plainly as you can get it before you reach for structure, because a structure built from a thin surface will be a guess.

2. Structure pass, Frame 2. For each surface expression, name the structural condition that would generate it: the arrangement underneath that the visible thing is an expression of. A recurring sanction for overuse is the surface of a graduated-response condition; a rotation is the surface of a contribution condition; a boundary marker is the surface of a membership condition. These named conditions are your candidate basis.

3. Reduce to a minimal basis with the lossless round-trip. A basis is right only when the whole surface can be rebuilt from it and every condition is doing generative work. Run the round-trip both ways: every surface expression should reconstruct from the conditions, and every condition should generate some part of the surface. If a surface expression cannot be rebuilt from the basis, the basis is missing a condition; add it. If a condition generates nothing on the surface, it is excess; this is where the sharper Occam's razor cuts. A basis that lists conditions but cannot regenerate the surface is a catalogue, not a basis, and a catalogue is the failure this step exists to catch.

4. The orientation check. Ask the question the machine cannot: is this the structure actually operating in this domain, or a structure I prefer that I am projecting onto it. This is the no-preference check, and it is yours to make. The strongest form of it is not introspection but grinding: take a rival decomposition built by someone who would structure the domain differently at the root, and run the disconfirmation pass. Where the two decompositions converge, you have something close to the real basis; where they diverge, one of you is imposing a preference, and you have to find which. If your basis converges with a specialist's independent account of the same domain, that convergence is corroboration, not coincidence.

5. Name the basis. Hold the names of the conditions to the vocabulary check, so the structure you surfaced is stated in terms that carry it rather than in borrowed vocabulary. The output is the domain's structural basis, named and minimal, which can then feed whatever you needed the structure for, including the per-axis measurement elements a domain-specific standard requires.

## A worked example: structuring a practice that has never been specified

A community manages a shared resource well, and no one has ever written down how. You need its structure, and there is no vocabulary for it.

Surface pass. You collect what actually happens: scheduled work sessions, a rotation for who draws how much, escalating responses when someone overdraws, markers of who is in and who is out, periodic sessions where disputes are settled cheaply and fast, a record of contributions.

Structure pass. Under each, the generating condition: the rotation is the surface of a graduated-contribution condition; the escalating responses, of a graduated-response-to-non-conformance condition; the in-and-out markers, of a defined-membership condition; the cheap dispute sessions, of a low-cost-conflict-resolution condition. Those four conditions are the candidate basis.

Round-trip. Each surface practice rebuilds from a condition, and each condition generates a practice, so the basis regenerates the surface. One practice, the contribution record, turns out to rebuild from the membership and contribution conditions together rather than needing its own, so it is not a fifth condition; that is the razor cutting a catalogue entry. Nothing on the surface fails to reconstruct, so the basis is complete.

Orientation check. Are these the conditions actually operating here, or am I laying a model I like over the community. You grind it against a rival who would decompose the same practice as a gift economy. Where you both arrive at the membership and conflict conditions from different roots, those are solid; where the rival sees something your model has no place for, you look again, and you find it or you correct your basis. If the conditions you surfaced converge with a specialist's independent design principles for managed commons, that convergence is corroboration that you found structure rather than projected it.

Name. You hold the condition names to the vocabulary check, so the structure is stated as named conditions with their mechanisms rather than as "good governance," and the basis is ready to use.

## How it chains

Decomposition is the front of the same chain the other guides run. Place each candidate account at its level so you are comparing structure to structure, decompose to a candidate basis, grind rival decompositions against each other to the basis they share, cut the catalogue entries with the razor, and name the survivors with the vocabulary check. The orientation check rides across the whole of it, because every step can quietly substitute a preference for a found structure, and only the human holding catches that.

## How it is misapplied, and how to catch it

Stopping at the surface. Listing the visible expressions and calling that the structure leaves you with Frame 1, a catalogue of what shows up with no account of what generates it. Catch: the structure pass; name the generating condition under each expression.

A catalogue instead of a basis. Conditions that do not regenerate the surface are a list, not a generative basis. Catch: the lossless round-trip, run both ways.

Imposing structure. Projecting a preferred model onto a domain that does not have it is the central failure, and it is invisible from inside your own account. Catch: the orientation check, made real by grinding against a rival decomposition rather than by introspection.

Composing toward the source. Treating decomposition as a way to reach the whole truth of the domain, the thing itself, overruns the method. It reaches the structural basis, optimal Frame 2, and no further. Catch: hold the build-versus-practice line; the basis is the most the method builds, and the orientation that keeps it honest is practiced, not built.

Over-decomposing. Splitting the basis into ever-finer conditions to look more rigorous cuts real structure into fragments that no longer generate cleanly. Catch: the razor's lossless-reconstruction guard; a condition that can be rebuilt from the others is excess, but one whose removal loses real generative power is load-bearing.

## How much runs inside AI

The decomposition to best Frame 2 can run inside an AI system: surfacing the expressions, proposing the generating conditions, running the round-trip, and grading the basis are conformance work, and a tool can carry them. What a machine cannot do is the orientation check. Whether a surfaced structure is found or imposed is the holding, the no-preference reading, and the corpus is explicit that the tool which mechanizes the building and the grading has no such check, because that judgment is not a grade. So automate the decomposition and the round-trip, and keep the orientation check, made real by an independent rival decomposition rather than by the same system agreeing with itself.

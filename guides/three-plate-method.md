# Three-Plate Method: Implementation Guide

## What this guide is, and what it is not

This is an implementation guide. It is how to actually run the three-plate method on a real problem, written for someone who will use the method rather than refine it.

The method takes its name from metrology. Grind three imperfect surfaces against each other in every pairing, rotating the work and iterating, and a true flat appears with no flat surface anywhere to copy from, because flatness is the only shape all three can share at once. Two surfaces will fool you: one slightly domed and one slightly hollowed mate perfectly and each certifies the other. Three cannot all mate unless all three are actually flat. The reference is produced from the mutual grinding alone, no external standard required. The method's application to frames, evaluations, and claims is named frame-primacy disconfirmation. This guide treats the two as one method seen in two materials.

The guide does not derive the method or argue its foundations. Those are in the two documents named in the status line. It shows three things: when the method is the right tool and when it is not, how to run it step by step, and the specific ways people misapply it.

One thing has to be held from the first line, because everything else rests on it. The method produces a very good Frame 2 and never more. It establishes what is actually there, the verified shared floor, and it does not, and cannot, deliver the worth, the value, or the right call. That verdict is a Frame 3 judgment no amount of grinding reaches, and it stays with the human actor. A guide that lets the reader forget this has already caused the misapplication it was meant to prevent. Where the method is helpful, and it is genuinely helpful, it is helpful as a way to reach a dependable floor, not as a way to settle worth.

## When to reach for it: the applicability test

Reach for the three-plate method only when all three of these hold at once.

1. You need a reference, and none can be inherited. There is no agreed external standard you could bring in and apply. If one exists, inherit it: use the standard, the way you would wring gauge blocks to a known dimension, and do not bootstrap a reference you already have. The three-plate method is for the case where the flat surface does not yet exist anywhere to copy.

2. A verified Frame 2 floor would actually solve your problem. What you need is to establish what is there, the shared invariant, and that is enough to act. If what you actually need is the worth-verdict, the value, the decision of which option is best, the method will not give it to you, and reaching for it there is the central misuse.

3. You can obtain at least three genuinely independent reads. Independent means they differ at the root, not three people from one team or trained in one frame. If you cannot get genuine independence, do not run the method, because it will produce a confident floor that is really a shared error, and a confident false floor is worse than no floor.

When any one of these fails, the method is the wrong tool. The misapplication section says what to do instead.

## How to run it

Six steps. The discipline is in steps two, three, and five, which is also where the failures cluster.

1. State the question as a floor, not a verdict. Write down what you are trying to establish as a matter of what is there: what this grant actually does, what this proposal actually coordinates, what this claim actually rests on. If your question contains the words worth, best, or should, you have written a verdict, and the method does not answer it. Rewrite it as a floor before you go on.

2. Assemble three or more genuinely independent reads. Independence is the load-bearing condition, so build it deliberately. Each read should come from a position that differs at the root from the others: a different discipline, a different incentive, a different frame, ideally one drawn from outside the body of people who built the thing. Climb the independence ladder as far as you can: a single in-context pass is the weakest plate, independent reviewers in prose are stronger, maximally different frames including ones from outside the field are stronger still, and constructed counterexamples and reads of the primary evidence are the hard plates that settle most. The test for whether a read is a separate plate is simple: it must name the independent surface it brings. Three reviewers who cannot each name what they see that the others do not are one surface wearing three costumes.

3. Grind across the full range, not at the summary. Do not compare three overall scores or three verdicts. Compare the reads claim by claim and axis by axis, through the whole object, because matched curvature passes at the summary level and only shows itself under the full pass. This is the rotating of the plates: you are looking for where the reads agree and disagree at every point, not in aggregate.

4. Read convergence as the floor and divergence as the contested map. What all the reads agree on, point by point, is the floor: this is what is actually there, verified, with no external standard required. What they disagree on is the contested region. Mark it as contested. Do not average it into a number, because averaging a divergence is the two-plate collusion dressed up as a score, and it buries exactly what the grind surfaced.

5. Stop at the dry round. The method is a reduction, and while it is reducing it is self-limiting: each round finds less, then nothing. The first round that brings no new independent surface and breaks nothing further is the dry round, and it is the end. Stop there. Past the floor the method inverts: a further round no longer removes a real excess, it manufactures a divergence against a sound result, and that fabricated divergence feels exactly like a finding. The rule that holds the line is one sentence: no new round without a new independent surface. A divergence having appeared is never by itself a reason to continue.

6. Emit the floor and the contested map, and hand the worth-judgment to the human. The output of a run is two things: the verified floor, and the contested region named as contested. It is not a score and not a verdict. The decision of what the thing is worth, whether to fund it, back it, or accept it, is a Frame 3 judgment that takes the floor and the contested map as honest inputs and is made by a person. The method did not withhold the verdict by accident; refusing it is what keeps the floor honest.

## A worked example: evaluating a grant where there is no standard

A grants round has to assess a proposal, and there is no agreed external measure of its impact to inherit. Today the gap is usually filled by a single reviewer's impression, which is one plate: its own reference, its error invisible from inside, a verdict asserted with nothing to check it against. Here is the same assessment run as a three-plate pass.

Step one, the question as a floor. Not "is this grant good and how much should it receive," which is a verdict, but "what does this proposal actually build, what does it actually coordinate, and what specific condition does it actually address."

Step two, three independent reads that differ at the root. A technical reviewer who reads the mechanism and asks only whether it does what it claims. Someone inside the domain the proposal touches, who asks whether it addresses a real and specific condition there, and whether its stated origin is specific or a vast FROM too broad to test. An adversarial skeptic who asks what would have to be true for this to fail and which claims are unsupported. Three surfaces, ground from three different roots, none of them from the proposing team.

Step three, grind across the full range. Each read passes over every claim: the mechanism, the parties coordinated, the stated origin condition, the outcome measure, the timeline. The reads are compared point by point.

Step four, the floor and the contested map. Suppose all three agree the proposal builds a specific mechanism, coordinates a specific set of parties, and addresses a specific named condition. That is the floor: it survived three independent reads, and it is now established without any external standard. Suppose they diverge elsewhere: the technical reviewer accepts the impact claim at face value, the domain reviewer flags the stated origin as a vast FROM that no specific intervention could be excluded by, and the skeptic finds the outcome measure unsupported by anything in the proposal. That divergence is the contested map. It is reported as contested, not blended into a confidence number.

Step five, the dry round. A fourth read from the same background as the technical reviewer adds no new surface and is not a new round. The run is over.

Step six, emit and hand off. The output is the verified floor, what the proposal actually builds and coordinates and addresses, and the contested map, the vast FROM and the unsupported outcome measure. The funding decision, what this is worth and at what level, is made by a person, with the floor and the contested map in front of them, and is not a score the method pretended to compute. The reviewer's old impression claimed to answer the worth question directly; the three-plate pass refuses that question and answers the one it can, with a rigor the single impression never had.

## Other places the same shape fits

The applicability test, not the grant example, is what tells you whether the method fits. Three other recurring cases pass it.

Decomposing a new domain's vocabulary when no canonical terms exist. Three independent decompositions of the domain, ground against each other: what they converge on is the load-bearing structure, what they diverge on is the contested or non-minimal part. This is frame-primacy disconfirmation in its original use, reducing a frame to its minimal basis.

Cross-checking a model output where there is no oracle. Three independent frames, ideally different base models or genuinely different prompts and not one model's restatements, are the three plates. The same independence rule binds hardest here, because a fan-out from one model is one surface in costumes and its agreement certifies nothing.

Diligence on a project that has no external audit. The team's own account, the on-chain or primary record, and an unaffiliated third-party read are ground together. The unsupported claims and the vast FROM are exactly the high spots that do not survive the grinding, and they end up in the contested map rather than the floor.

## How it is misapplied, and how to catch it

Each entry is a misuse and the catch for it. These are the failures that make a confident floor that is really a shared error, which is the one outcome worse than not running the method at all.

Two reads instead of three. Two reviewers who agree feel like confirmation. They are not: two surfaces can be complementary curves that mate perfectly while neither is flat. Catch: require three at minimum, and require them to differ at the root.

Same-mold reads, or costumes. Three reviewers from one team or one training, or one person generating three rival frames, are one surface wearing several costumes, and their agreement certifies nothing. This is the two-matched-curves failure raised a level. Catch: require each read to name the independent surface it brings; if it cannot, it is not a separate plate, and the count of real plates is lower than it looks.

Grinding at the summary level. Comparing three overall scores hides matched curvature, which only shows under the full pass. Catch: grind claim by claim and axis by axis, never verdict against verdict.

Running past the floor. After the picture is clear, one more round to tighten it finds a fresh disagreement that feels like a finding. Past the floor the method manufactures divergence. Catch: stop at the dry round, and take no new round without a new independent surface; a divergence is not by itself a reason to continue.

Averaging the divergence into a number. Turning the contested region into a single confidence figure or a blended score is the two-plate collusion dressed as a number, and it buries what the grind surfaced. Catch: report the contested region as contested and let the human weigh it.

Expecting a Frame 3 verdict. Asking the method whether the grant is good, the project worth backing, or the answer correct is asking the plane to be the source. The method establishes the floor, not the worth. Catch: hold that the output is always "here is what is verified and here is what is contested," and that the worth-judgment is the human's and was never the method's to give.

Curating which reads count. Dropping the read that disagrees, or choosing reviewers likely to converge, is grinding a rigged set of plates and will produce whatever floor you selected for. Catch: fix the set before the grind, and treat an inconvenient divergence as signal rather than as a read to discard. Test the exclusions, not only the inclusions.
